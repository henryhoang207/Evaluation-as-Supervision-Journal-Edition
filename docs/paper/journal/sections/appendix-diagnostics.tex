\section{Predictions and exploratory diagnostics}
\label{app:diagnostics}
\subsection{Pre-recorded predictions}

Table~\ref{tab:predictions} sets the results against the directional
predictions recorded before any trial. The capability ceiling and the
undetectable C1 interaction came out as predicted. The repair rates did not.
The predictions for \arm{A1} (about 45\%) and \arm{A2} (about 33\%) came from
code-repair benchmarks with execution feedback
\citep{arimbur2026,chen2024selfdebug}. Both arms instead sit near 94\%. The
predicted class ordering is reversed: referential defects (\op{O1}, \op{O2}),
expected to be the easiest, were the hardest, and only \op{O1} has real
headroom.

\paragraph{Search space across review-count strata}
Differences in prompt search space may contribute to the unexpected class ordering; these post-hoc strata are small and do not establish a mechanism. Workloads in the study are
stratified by review count into quartiles plus the 90th percentile:
$p_{25}=14, p_{50}=34, p_{75}=92, p_{90}=214$ reviews. Only prompts at the
reasoning stage (\op{O1}--\op{O3}) contain the analysed review records and scale in
length with review count $N$. Downstream stages (\op{O5}--\op{O7}) receive only
compact summaries of 3--6 items and serve as flat controls.

Operator-specific patterns are informative because their search demands differ:
\begin{itemize}
\item \op{O1} (deleting 1 character from a 22-character review identifier):
  repair requires retrieving the exact original identifier from the $N$ reviews
  in the prompt under both \arm{A1} and \arm{A2}. On the hosted model
  (\cell{T2}), repair remains saturated (83.3\%--100.0\%) across $p_{25}$--$p_{75}$,
  but drops steeply at $p_{90}$ (214 reviews) to \textbf{46.7\%} for \arm{A1}
  and \textbf{26.7\%} for \arm{A2} (log-slope $-0.100$ \ci{-0.269}{+0.088} on
  \arm{A1}, $-0.215$ \ci{-0.341}{-0.062} on \arm{A2}). This 46.7\% cliff
  replicates identically in the \cell{T2S} severity pass across both severity
  levels (level~1: 46.7\%; level~2: 46.7\%).
\item \op{O2} (appending a fabricated review identifier): repair requires
  deleting the fake identifier. Under \arm{A1}, the feedback explicitly names the
  fabricated identifier, so the named item is available in feedback. Under
  \arm{A2}, the feedback provides only a generic rejection, which does not identify the offending item. On the local model
  (Qwen2.5-7B, \cell{T2L}), the observed rates differ: \arm{A1} shows no clear stratum trend
  across review-count strata (66.7\% $\rightarrow$ 83.3\% $\rightarrow$ 42.9\%
  $\rightarrow$ 66.7\%, slope $-0.074$ \ci{-0.314}{+0.141}), whereas \arm{A2}
  collapses monotonically (44.4\% $\rightarrow$ 33.3\% $\rightarrow$ 14.3\%
  $\rightarrow$ \textbf{0.0\%} at $p_{90}$, slope $-0.165$ \ci{-0.331}{+0.000}).
  These patterns are exploratory and based on few defects per stratum.
\item \op{O3} (assigning an incorrect aspect label): verifying which evidence
  review mentions which aspect also scales with $N$, declining on Qwen2.5-7B
  from 100.0\% ($p_{25}$) to \textbf{0.0\%} ($p_{90}$, slope $-0.234$
  \ci{-0.349}{-0.051}).
\item Controls (\op{O5}, \op{O6}, \op{O7}): upstream prompts contain no raw
  reviews, and repair rates are relatively high at the largest stratum
  (75\%--100\% at $p_{90}$) on both models.
\end{itemize}

\noindent Furthermore, on the hosted model (\cell{T2}), even where overall
repair rates remain near 100\%, exact restoration to the clean output
is lower at the largest stratum for all three reasoning operators:
\op{O1} falls from 66.7\% ($p_{25}$) to \textbf{0.0\%} ($p_{90}$, slope $-0.221$
\ci{-0.390}{-0.023}); \op{O2} falls from 94.4\% to \textbf{33.3\%} (slope
$-0.247$ \ci{-0.376}{-0.115}); and \op{O3} falls from 100.0\% to \textbf{0.0\%}
(slope $-0.343$ \ci{-0.441}{-0.211}). By contrast, the report-stage control
\op{O6} shows a less pronounced decline (100.0\% $\rightarrow$ 100.0\% $\rightarrow$ 90.5\%
$\rightarrow$ 83.3\%, slope $-0.066$).

\subsection{Trial-level decomposition}
\paragraph{Descriptive within-rewrite repair rates}
Restricting repair rates to trials that did \emph{not} reproduce the clean
output yields 83.5\% (111 of 133) for \arm{A1} versus 87.1\% (169 of 194) for
\arm{A2} on the hosted model, and 53.3\% (128 of 240) versus 44.4\% (111 of 250)
on Qwen2.5-7B. We report these within-rewrite numbers strictly descriptively,
without causal interpretation. Conditioning on non-identical outputs introduces
potential selection bias: the composition of non-identical subsets can differ across arms.
